# Patches to the submodules

Changes to `third_party/wifi-sdr` and its `external/devourer` that are not (yet) upstream. They come from
[Play RX](https://github.com/scerstt/PlayRX) (GPL-2.0, like fpvDROID), a Windows receiver built on kepyur, and
were regenerated against the submodule commits this repository pins. The wifi-sdr patch also carries two
additions of fpvDROID's own, on top of Play RX's content, which is otherwise as Play RX has it.

| Patch | Applies in | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| `wifi-sdr-0001-playrx-stream.patch` | `third_party/wifi-sdr` | `RxSettings::centre` (put the hardware centre beside the pilot, off the chip's DC point; `shift_hz` stays `freq - centre`); late or short banks in the first few of a run dropped instead of restarting the stream (behind a USB hub the first bank ships late every time); mid-run, an occasional late or overrun bank dropped instead of a restart (a burst still ends the run); `request_usb3(..., force)`; `WSDR_URB_DEPTH` and `WSDR_FW_TABLE_OFLD` overrides, the firmware table offload on by default only on Windows. **fpvDROID's additions** (comments marked `fpvDROID:`): the banks a run drops are reported while it runs, in `RxStatus::mid_drops` and `RxStatus::start_late_skips` (through `StreamCfg::on_drop`), and `BankMeta::seq` steps over them. Without it nothing above the stream could tell that banks were missing: a run that keeps dropping one now and then never ends, so never returns its `StreamStats`. And the C2H wedge (wifi-sdr's FINDINGS §4: on some USB 3 hosts the chip's C2H engine stops taking requests) is freed without opening the adapter again. What frees it is the RX DMA enables in REG_CR (0x100 bits 1 and 3) off and on: found on a Pixel 8 Pro by giving each of 93 wedges one of nine candidates in turn (this one 13 of 13; a new start alone, a USB port reset and the others 0 of 13). The onboard program (`src/fw/stream8822b.S`, `SEND`) gives that pulse itself when a request has been pending for `StreamCfg::unwedge_us` (100 us on Android at USB 3, off elsewhere; `WSDR_WEDGE_US` sets it, 0 for off), and counts its pulses in the bank's trailer, which is 4 bytes longer for it. The packet it frees comes with 8192 bytes too many, as a 16384-byte and an 8184-byte transfer, and the stream puts it back together: at 20 MS/s, where 97 % of the wedges are at a bank's first packet, the bank still arrives in time and nothing is lost (334 wedges in 148 s: 322 so, 12 at a later packet, which cost a new start of ~40 ms; none an open of ~3 s). At 40 MS/s a bank has no time to spare for it, so a wedge there costs a new start; there are far fewer of them (about 10 in 190 s, against about 100 a minute at 20 MS/s), and in three minutes at each rate the stream delivered 99.7 % and 99.6 % of its banks. The pulse also fires when the host stops reading for more than `unwedge_us` (96 times in one 10 ms stall), and two ways of holding it back were tried against forced stalls of ~0.5 to 1.2 ms at 20 MS/s (about 46 a setting; the trial's sources are not in the tree): at most one pulse a request was no better (71 % of the stalls ended the run, against 65 %), and no pulse while RXDMA_REQ (REG 0x284 BIT19) reads 1 was better there (48 %) but had 11 new starts in its 88 s check run against 4 in 193 s without it, so neither is in. With no pulse at all 76 % of the stalls ended the run, most of them as a wedge: the chip wedged about 15 times as often as usual while the host was not collecting its transfers. What sets the wedge off was then tried from both ends, at 20 MS/s in settings of 30 s (also not in the tree). When the host resubmits its transfers matters: 100 to 136 wedges a minute as the stream thread does it (it sleeps between packets); 284 when it collects each packet at once and so resubmits the bank's last transfer a full idle gap before the next bank; 88 with three more resubmits spread through the gap, no better than as it is; 1301 with the last two held back until the next bank starts. And the idle gap matters: with the chip pausing between packets so that a bank's five start 60 us apart, 16 wedges a minute, and 88 us apart (no gap over ~55 us), 2. But those two settings had 10 and 9 new starts in their 30 s, against 1 to 5 as it is and none in the two settings that collect at once: packets that come as a 16384-byte transfer with no 8184-byte one after it, which the stream did not then put back together, so spreading the packets cost more than it saved. Ten of those were then recorded (that transfer and the four after it), and all ten are one shape: K bytes, then a whole 16376-byte packet starting at K, as a 16384-byte transfer and one of K - 8, with the stream in step after it. In seven the K bytes are the packet's own start over again (K 6144, 13312 or 14336 where the wedged packet has 8192): the pulse came when the packet was already well on its way, and the packet at K is whole. In three K is 15360, the bytes before it are the packet before, short of its last 1016, and the chip had given no pulse: that packet is lost (a part 3 so cut comes with the trailer straight after it, 15532 bytes), the one at K is whole. The stream now puts the first kind back together at any K and drops only the bank of a packet cut short (`StreamStats::cut`), where each cost a new start. With that, 55 s at 20 MS/s: 127 wedges freed, three packets put back together from 13312, 5120 and 6144 bytes in, one bank dropped for a cut packet, no new start (nor in two runs of 50 s before it; as it was, 1 to 5 in 30 s). Spreading the packets was tried again with it (50 s each): 2 wedges and one bank dropped with, 82 wedges and one bank dropped without, no new start either way. It leaves a bank ~85 us to spare instead of ~215 and gains nothing more, so it is not in. At 40 MS/s the packets run back to back and a bank has ~14 us to spare (it ends 171 us after due of the 185 allowed), so a packet put back together there costs its bank and the one or two after it. Eleven new starts at that rate were recorded: eight were the host pausing for 1 to 4 ms with the onboard program pulsing all the while (bare 24-byte descriptors and pieces of packets come out), and three a 134 to 138-byte packet that is none of the stream's but has the C2H bit in byte 11. So a transfer of none of the stream's lengths is now skipped whatever that bit says, and so is a 16384-byte transfer that the rest of a packet does not follow (more than 256 in one bank still end the run); the bank they were in comes short and is dropped. With that, 3 minutes at 20 MS/s: 364 wedges freed, four packets put back together at other cut points, one bank dropped, no new start (about 15 minutes at 20 MS/s in all since the wider re-join: one new start, when the host stopped reading for 4.4 ms). With 40 MS/s chosen, 2 minutes: 5 new starts and 25 banks dropped; in a 4 minute stream test, 7 and 92. Every new start left at 40 MS/s is the burst rule (more than 4 banks dropped within 64). One thing changes for the app: lost blocks at 40 MS/s now come in ones to fours where a new start came before, and that can reach the 10 in 5 s at which a 40 MS/s watch goes on at 20 MS/s (`native/radio/fallback_policy.h`): it did in one run of three, 20 s in. A wedge that gets past the onboard program is recognised as before (a packet does not come and the 0x294 request is still pending): the run pulses the RX DMA on its way out, the Receiver gives it one new start, and only a wedge that comes back before a bank ends it with `RxStatus::wedged`, for the owner to open the adapter again. `RxStatus::unwedged` counts both kinds. `WSDR_USB3_PHY=1` writes the USB 3 PHY value Realtek's drivers write and devourer does not (PHY register 0x01 = 0xA841, cut D; `=0` only logs what is there): tried against the wedge on the same phone, no effect. A restart after a fault pauses 10 ms between starts instead of 100, and a start drains for 10 ms instead of 30: the hole a restart leaves in the stream goes from ~200 ms to ~55 ms (measured on a Pixel 8 Pro, which restarts every few seconds at 40 MS/s), with no more failed starts. And whether the link is SuperSpeed comes from the descriptors when the kernel cannot say (Session::open: libusb asks with USBDEVFS_GET_SPEED, which older kernels lack, an LG G7 on Android 10 for one): taken as USB 2, a SuperSpeed link got 4096-byte EP0 writes, which the chip stalls (LIBUSB_ERROR_PIPE at every start); a bulk endpoint's 1024-byte packets now say USB 3 |
| `devourer-0001-playrx-usb.patch` | `third_party/wifi-sdr/external/devourer` | control reads retried with a back-off; a circuit breaker that fails every control transfer at once after 8 failures in a row (or one NO_DEVICE), instead of minutes of retries on a vanished adapter; on Windows only: synchronous EP0 writes and retried writes |

The `#ifdef _WIN32` parts are inert on Android, Linux and the web build.

## Applying

The build applies them: `native/CMakeLists.txt` and `web/CMakeLists.txt` include `native/cmake/patches.cmake`,
which applies each patch unless it is already in, and stops the configure if one neither applies nor is in (the
submodule pin moved without the patch). To build wifi-sdr some other way, run `scripts/apply-patches.sh` first:
it runs the same file as a script (`cmake -P native/cmake/patches.cmake`).
`.gitmodules` has `ignore = dirty` for wifi-sdr, so the patched working trees do not show in `git status`.

It works on the sources as plain directories too (no submodule metadata: an archive, or fpvDROID copied into
another repository), and on a copy of a checkout made without its top `.git` directory, whose submodules still
have a `.git` file that now leads nowhere. It checks after the fact that a line each patch adds is really in
the tree: the marker, at the end of `patches.cmake`. A patch that adds or removes that line needs its marker
changed with it.

## Regenerating one

After changing the patched code, or after moving a submodule pin and fixing whatever no longer applies:

    git -C third_party/wifi-sdr diff --ignore-submodules > patches/wifi-sdr-0001-playrx-stream.patch
    git -C third_party/wifi-sdr/external/devourer diff --ignore-submodules > patches/devourer-0001-playrx-usb.patch
